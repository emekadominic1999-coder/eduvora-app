A simple weekly study timetable for university students

By Eduvora · Updated 12 October 2026

A good timetable is not a perfect plan. It is a plan you will actually follow. The goal is to make studying a regular habit instead of a panic before tests.

Start with fixed commitments

Write down everything that cannot move: lectures, labs, tutorials, church or mosque, work, travel time and meals. Then look at what is left. Those free blocks are your study time. Many students plan as if they have all day, then wonder why the plan fails.

Use short, regular sessions

Most people concentrate well for 25 to 50 minutes, then drift. Work in blocks of that length with a 5 to 10 minute break between them. Two focused hours usually beat five distracted ones. Put your phone out of reach during each block.

Follow the same-day review rule

The most useful habit is spending 20 to 30 minutes on the same evening reviewing what you were taught that day. Rewrite key points in your own words and list anything unclear to ask about. This takes little time and saves hours of relearning later.

Add spaced revision

Memory fades unless you revisit material. Return to each topic after a day, then after about a week, then before the test. Short quizzes work better than rereading notes, because they make your brain retrieve the answer.

A sample week

This is only an example. Adjust it to your own lectures.

DayMorning/afternoonEvening
MondayLecturesSame-day review (30 min), then Course A reading (1 hr)
TuesdayLecturesSame-day review, then Course B problems (1 hr)
WednesdayLectures and labsSame-day review, then Course C notes (1 hr)
ThursdayLecturesSame-day review, then a short quiz on Courses A and B
FridayLecturesLight review, then rest
SaturdayTwo 90-minute study blocks for your hardest courseFree time
SundayOne block of weekly review and planningRest, prepare for Monday

Balance the courses

Give more time to courses with more credit units and to those you find hardest. Do not spend all your time on the courses you enjoy. A quick way to decide is to rank your courses by units and by difficulty, then share your free blocks accordingly. Our guide on course outlines explains how to see the units.

Protect sleep, food and rest

Late-night cramming reduces recall. Aim for regular sleep, real meals and at least one proper break each week. Rest is part of the plan, not a reward for finishing it.

Adjusting for test and exam season

About two weeks before assessments, shift the balance. Keep the same-day review, but replace some reading blocks with timed practice and with fixing the mistakes it reveals. In the final few days, cut new material, revisit your short notes and protect your sleep. If a clash appears, such as two tests on one day, move revision for the later one earlier in the week instead of squeezing both into one evening.

Keeping to it

  • Plan tomorrow tonight. Choose the first task before you sleep.
  • Start with a small, easy task to get going.
  • If you miss a session, do not abandon the week. Move it to the next free block.
  • Review the timetable every Sunday and adjust it. A plan that never changes is probably not being used.
