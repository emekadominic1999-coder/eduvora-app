How to read and use a university course outline

By Eduvora · Updated 12 October 2026

A course outline is the list of courses your department expects you to take, level by level. It looks dry, but it is one of the most useful documents you have. It tells you what to register, what each course is worth and what you must pass to graduate.

Decode the course code

A code such as MTH 101 usually has two parts. The letters identify the department or subject area, and the number carries information too. In many Nigerian universities the first digit shows the level, so 1xx is a 100-level course, 2xx is 200-level, and so on. The remaining digits distinguish individual courses. Conventions vary between schools, so check how yours works.

Understand credit units

Each course has a credit unit value, often between one and four. Units show how much weight the course carries both in workload and in your GPA. A 4-unit course affects your GPA twice as much as a 2-unit one, so it deserves more study time. See our GPA guide for the maths.

Know the types of course

  • Compulsory or core courses: you must take and pass them for your degree.
  • Elective courses: you choose from a list, often to reach a required number of units.
  • General studies courses: taken by students across departments, often covering communication, ICT or entrepreneurship. These carry units and can affect your CGPA, so they deserve real effort.
  • Ancillary or service courses: taught by another department but required in yours, such as mathematics for science students.

Check prerequisites and semester placement

Some courses cannot be taken until you have passed an earlier one. Others are offered only in the first or second semester. A missed prerequisite can delay graduation, so look ahead a full year, not only at the current semester.

Work out your unit load

Schools set a minimum and maximum number of units per semester. Add up the units of the courses you plan to register and confirm that the total sits within your school's limits. Carryovers from earlier semesters count towards this load as well.

Use the outline to plan your study

  1. List your courses for the semester with their units.
  2. Split the topics. Ask lecturers or coursemates for the topic list of each course and divide it by the weeks left.
  3. Prioritise by weight. Spend more time on high-unit courses and on courses you find hard.
  4. Track progress. Tick off each topic after you can explain it without notes.

Common mistakes

  • Relying on an outline from a senior student without checking that it still applies to your session. Curricula get revised.
  • Ignoring general studies courses until the last minute.
  • Registering courses without checking the unit limits.
  • Forgetting carryovers.

Your department's official document is always the authority. If an outline you find online disagrees with it, follow your department and ask your course adviser.
